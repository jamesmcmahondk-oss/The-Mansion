## Phillip “Pip” Jackson

![Portrait of Phillip “Pip” Jackson clutching a teddy bear in a candlelit Gothic interior.](characters-assets/pip.jpg)

**The quiet resident with Bear** · *Established canon*

> Fragile-looking, frightened and peculiar—and not a puzzle that needs solving.

**Backstory.** Pip is a vulnerable resident who clings to familiar things, especially a very old teddy bear. The gallery records him only as an informal study: “Phillip Jackson and bear. Not commissioned. Preserved by request.” His story deliberately has no secret crown, cursed inheritance or grand Gothic tragedy. Bear matters because Pip cares about Bear; Pip matters because Pip exists.

**Story tension.** Other people may mistake vulnerability for evidence of a hidden catastrophe. Pip quietly refuses that assumption.

## Erasmus

![Portrait of Erasmus the Wizard, an elderly bearded scholar carrying books, scrolls and diagrams.](characters-assets/erasmus.jpg)

**The Wizard** · *Portrait-led profile*

> A scholar carrying more answers than he can comfortably organise.

**Backstory.** Erasmus is identified by the portrait as the Mansion’s wizard. Books, star charts and loose papers mark him as a learned researcher rather than a battlefield sorcerer, while his bewildered expression keeps that learning human. The supplied story material does not yet establish his origin, allegiance or defining mistake, so those details remain deliberately open.

**Story tension.** Knowledge is his authority—and, judging by the paperwork, possibly his burden.

## Cairn the Steadfast

![Portrait of Cairn the Steadfast, the horned stone gargoyle of the Front Gate.](characters-assets/cairn.jpg)

**Front Gate Guardian · formerly known only as “Sad Gargoyle”** · *Established canon*

> Loyalty held so long that it became a prison.

**Backstory.** Cairn and Dúnán were commissioned as a pair to guard the Front Gate. Long after the old duty had lost its meaning, Cairn remained on the pillar: not because stone or magic held him there, but because he had been told to remain. Habit, pride, loneliness and fear of choosing hardened into obligation. His grand name eventually fell out of use and survives in the Portrait Ledger.

**Story tension.** Cairn must learn that duty is meaningful only when it is still chosen.

## Hautain

![Portrait of Hautain the Frog King, bruised but crowned and holding a jeweled sceptre.](characters-assets/hautain.jpg)

**The Frog King** · *Partly established*

> Bruised dignity, an undiminished crown and a kingdom that still needs a witness.

**Backstory.** Hautain is a crowned frog monarch whose beaten portrait proves that a confrontation has physically happened without stripping him of ceremony. His danger can be comic in a way Lady Vesper’s is not, but the joke should not erase the need beneath the pageantry: Hautain needs someone to acknowledge that his kingdom still exists.

**Story tension.** Is a kingdom still a kingdom when nobody remains to recognise it?

## Magnus

![Portrait of Magnus the Head Cook laughing in the Mansion kitchen with a ladle and copper pot.](characters-assets/magnus.jpg)

**The Head Cook** · *Portrait-led profile*

> Warmth, abundance and instinct at the heart of the kitchen.

**Backstory.** Magnus is identified as the Mansion’s Head Cook. His stained whites, raised ladle and broad laugh suggest a practical cook who measures success by the pot, the table and the people fed. The supplied material does not yet establish how he came to the Mansion or how his title relates to Séverin’s, leaving their shared kitchen history available for later canon.

**Story tension.** His portrait promises generosity and improvisation—but not yet what happens when those instincts meet Séverin’s discipline.

<a id="mirelle"></a>
## Mirélle

![Portrait of Mirélle the Fair Witch in an elaborate feathered hat with crystals and jewelry.](characters-assets/mirelle.jpg)

**The Fair Witch** · *Established canon*

> She is not afraid of losing beauty; she is afraid that change means losing herself.

**Backstory.** Mirélle commissioned portraits across decades—in 1798, 1821 and 1854—and rejected every version that admitted time had passed. She surrounds herself with immaculate clothes, jewels and a flawless hand mirror, using presentation and magic to preserve the person she remembers. Her eventual resolution is not a fall from beauty: it is the discovery that age did not make Mirélle disappear.

**Story tension.** She must recognise that changing and ceasing to exist are not the same thing.

## Quazimodo

![Portrait of Quazimodo the Butler in worn black formalwear inside the Mansion.](characters-assets/quazimodo.jpg)

**The Butler** · *Portrait-led profile*

> The Mansion’s formal face, weathered but still at his post.

**Backstory.** Quazimodo is identified as the Mansion’s butler. His frayed evening clothes and guarded expression suggest long service in a house whose routines have outlasted their original purpose. Existing notes confirm that physical events can leave him with a beaten-state portrait, but the supplied material does not yet define his life before service or the incident that produces that state.

**Story tension.** A servant who has kept the house functioning may know where every secret is kept—and still have no life outside the role.

<a id="severin"></a>
## Séverin

![Portrait of Séverin the Head Chef holding a recipe book in the Mansion kitchen.](characters-assets/severin.jpg)

**The Head Chef** · *Portrait-led profile*

> Recipes. Measure. Discipline. Perfection.

**Backstory.** Séverin is identified as the Mansion’s Head Chef. He appears as a severe technician: tasting spoon in one hand and a book literally devoted to recipes, measure, discipline and perfection in the other. The supplied story material has not yet fixed his earlier history or his relationship with Magnus, so the obvious contrast between their portraits remains a strong possibility rather than settled canon.

**Story tension.** Perfection can preserve standards; it can also leave no room for appetite, accident or joy.

## Sola

![Portrait of Sola the long-haired gray cat sitting on books beside an overturned goblet.](characters-assets/sola.jpg)

**The Cat** · *Portrait-led profile*

> A cat with a monogrammed collar and no apparent need to justify being here.

**Backstory.** Sola is, canonically, the cat. The portrait places Sola among books, instruments and an overturned goblet, wearing a medallion marked “S” and the expression of someone who considers the room correctly arranged. No supernatural origin or secret office is established in the supplied material, and none is needed for this guide.

**Story tension.** If Sola knows more than the residents, Sola is under no obligation to explain it.

## Sir Audax Fireheart

![Portrait of Sir Audax Fireheart, a small armored mouse knight with sword, shield and red plume.](characters-assets/audax.jpg)

**The Mouse Knight** · *Portrait-led profile*

> Small in stature, entirely full-sized in courage.

**Backstory.** Sir Audax Fireheart is presented as a fully titled knight: armored, plumed, sword raised and shield bearing a golden lion. The portrait establishes courage, ceremony and scale-defying confidence, but the supplied story material does not yet identify his campaign, oath or connection to the Mansion. He should not be confused with Valorian, the other knight mentioned in discussion.

**Story tension.** His name promises bravery; the unanswered question is what—or whom—he has sworn to defend.

<a id="dunan"></a>
## Dúnán the Patient

![Portrait card of Dúnán the Patient, a winged stone gargoyle perched high above the Mansion estate.](characters-assets/dunan.jpg)

**Former Front Gate Guardian · now on the roof** · *Established canon*

> He discovered that he could leave—and then discovered how easy it is to leave tomorrow.

**Backstory.** Dúnán guarded the Front Gate opposite Cairn for centuries. He eventually realised that neither gargoyle was physically bound to the pillars, tried and failed to persuade Cairn, and simply climbed down. Yet he never left the estate: he moved to a roof or tower with a better view and still watches the gate. Freedom replaced duty, but patience curdled into postponement.

**Story tension.** Dúnán must learn that freedom unused is another kind of imprisonment.

## Two story artifacts

These are not characters, but they connect several of the backstories above.

### The Worn Photograph

![A tiny worn photograph of several people posed on the Mansion steps.](characters-assets/worn-photograph.jpg)

An old image of several people on the Mansion steps. The player recognises the place, not the people. Its handwritten clue—“YOU SAID YOU’D COME BACK.”—connects the player to an absent identity without yet explaining who is missing.

### The Portrait Ledger

![The candlelit Portrait Gallery with a hidden ledger compartment open behind a frame.](characters-assets/portrait-ledger.jpg)

Hidden behind the opened frame in the Portrait Gallery, the ledger preserves what memory and habit have blurred: Mirélle’s rejected portraits, the paired commission of Dúnán and Cairn, and a damaged family record whose last name cannot be read.
